# The first run: June to August 2026

A remote consumer-tech company engaged MPIV through a talent marketplace to assess how employees find current information and to recommend a knowledge-management approach. Client name, figures, and people are removed. Counts of sources, dates, and the shape of the work are as they happened.

## Budget

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Weekly cap | 20 hours, with some weeks authorized to 30 |
| Length | Eight weeks, June to early August 2026 |
| Deliverable date | 4 August 2026 |
| Passes | Records and inventory (weeks 1 to 2), interviews (weeks 2 to 5), workspace and chat review (weeks 4 to 6), vendor sessions (weeks 5 to 8), writing (weeks 7 to 8) |

## Sources, as inventoried

| Source | Intake | What it covered | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| 13 champion interviews across functions | Call transcripts, summarized same day | Working perspectives, where handoffs fail | Raw transcripts excluded from the deliverable; summaries in an appendix |
| Application registry | MCP read of the live workspace | Tools the company knew about | Counts a different population than discovery |
| Automated discovery data, two dates | Export | Applications used with company email | Several hundred records unresolved; no seat or cost data |
| Workspace review across nine functions | MCP reads | Where each team keeps its record | A sample, not a full export |
| Five published remote-company practices | Web | Operating rules that work elsewhere | Guides rules; does not prove a vendor fits |
| Three vendor sessions and one prototype | Calls, demos | Fit of candidate tools | Production approval depended on launch checks listed in the deliverable |
| One requested export | Never arrived | Seat and licence use | Stayed open; named in the limits section |

Most material came in through MCP connections to the client's workspace and through call transcripts. The intake was incremental: sources arrived over six weeks and the findings table was rebuilt as they did.

## What the table produced

Five findings, each with evidence and an effect, became five deliverables: current state, peer benchmark, future-state architecture, roadmap, governance model. Four appendices carried the evidence summary, the discovery supporting work, the interview summaries and framework, and the vendor evaluation. The evidence summary opened with an "evidence used" table (source, what it covered, limit) and a "findings and their effect" table.

One finding pattern, with the client's figures removed: the registry and the discovery data counted different populations. The deliverable stated both counts with their populations and said what they did not show (active seats, duplicate licences, contract value, savings). Without that sentence the figures read as a savings claim.

## What broke or nearly did

- A requested export was due in week three and never came. Because the inventory carried the gap from that week, the final document said what stayed open instead of guessing.
- Interview summaries written days after the call lost the quotes. The same-day rule came from that.
- Early drafts edited prose directly; findings drifted from their evidence. The table-then-document rule came from that.

## Hours

The ledger was kept weekly against the 20-hour cap. Overage weeks used the authorized 30. The inventory and the passes were fixed before the reading started. Late sources went into the table without a rewrite of the document.
